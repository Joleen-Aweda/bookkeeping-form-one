This means that assets that take a shorter time to be converted into cash are considered to be more liquid than those that take a longer time.

Liquidity:

It is a measure of the ability of a business to meet its financial obligations as they fall due. One of the indicators of liquidity is time spent to convert its assets into cash.

The first approach commonly used to presenting the assets and liabilities is to list the assets and liabilities in order of liquidity. The second method involves the grouping of assets and liabilities by considering whether they are current or non-current items.

Listing in the order of liquidity

This approach is mostly used by financial institutions such as banks. Using this approach, the business lists its assets starting from the most liquid assets or liabilities to the least liquid ones. Alternatively, the assets and liabilities can be listed starting with the least liquid to the most liquid ones. Under this approach, liabilities will be listed starting with those that take the shortest time to be settled to the ones that take the longest time to be settled, and vice versa.

Example 7.5

Listing of assets and liabilities from the most liquid to the least liquid can be illustrated as follows:

Assets

  • Cash in hand
  • Cash at bank
  • Accounts receivable
  • Inventory
  • Furniture
  • Plant and machinery
  • Building
  • Land

Liabilities

  • Bank overdraft
  • Accounts payable
  • Long term loans
