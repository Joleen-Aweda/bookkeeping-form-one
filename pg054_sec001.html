Purchase returns daybook (purchase returns journal)

The purchase returns daybook is used to record transactions related to purchase returns, or return of goods to suppliers who supplied goods on credit (creditors). Each entry made include details of the goods and the credit note, the name of supplier (creditor) and the amount involved as well as the reason of returning the goods.

Cash book

The cash book is used to record transactions related to receipt and payment of cash as well as money placed into the bank (bank deposits) and those taken from the bank (bank withdrawals).

A cash book is a book of prime entry used to record transactions that involve movement of cash, that is receiving or paying cash by the business. Since cash is an asset for the business, receipts of cash are recorded by a debit entry in the cash book (increase in assets), and payment of cash is recorded by a credit entry in the cash book (decrease in asset). The cash book has a unique characteristic because, apart from being classified as a book of prime entry, it also forms part of a general ledger. This is because some small businesses that operate strictly on cash basis find it easy to skip journals and instead, enter all transactions (receipts and payments) in the cash book. For such businesses, when completing the corresponding entries to the other ledger accounts; they refer to that as posting.

A cash book is a financial record used to track cash and bank transactions in a business or enterprise. It serves as a primary accounting register for cash receipts and cash payments. The cash book records all inflows and outflows of cash, providing a complete and up-to-date record of the enterprise's cash position. The cash book can be maintained in both manual and electronic formats. In manual systems, it is often a physical book where transactions are recorded by hand, whereas in electronic systems, it is a digital record within a specific accounting software or any electronic spreadsheet. A cash book can take different forms ranging from single column cash book, two column cash book, three column cash book and petty cash book. For the purpose of this book, the focus will be on single column cash book.

Single column cash book

This cash book has a single column used to record all cash or bank transactions. Cash receipts, such as sales or loans received, are recorded on the debit side (left hand side) of the book. While cash payments, such as expenses or loan repayments, are recorded on the credit side (right hand side) of the book.

General journal (journal proper)
