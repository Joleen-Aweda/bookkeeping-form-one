Creditors

Creditors or payables are suppliers of goods or services, who sell goods or provide services to the business on credit. Creditors are interested in the information presented in the financial statements because they want to assess the ability of debtors to pay for the goods and services supplied on credit when due.

Debtors

Debtors or receivables are enterprises or individuals who purchase goods or services from the business on credit. Debtors do not usually use the financial statements of their creditors, but sometimes they need them (especially cash flow statements) to check the capability of these creditors to regularly supply goods especially when dealing with tender businesses.

Summary

Basic financial statements are key documents that provide a comprehensive overview of a business financial performance and position. These statements are crucial for stakeholders, including investors, creditors, and managers, to assess the business financial health and make informed decisions. Financial statements include among others, statement of financial position (balance sheet), income statement, and cash flow statement.

The statement of financial position presents a summary of the enterprise's financial position at a specific point in time, detailing its assets, liabilities, and equity. It demonstrates the accounting equation (Assets = Liabilities + Equity) and shows how the business’ resources are financed. The income statement, on the other hand, presents the businesses revenues, expenses, and net income (or net loss) over a specific period, typically a quarter or a year. It reveals the businesses profitability by comparing revenue with expenses. The cash flow statement illustrates the business cash inflows and outflows during a given period. It categorising them into operating, investing, and financing activities. This statement shows the business ability to generate and manage cash, which is vital for its sustainability and growth. Together, these basic financial statements provide a clear and comprehensive picture of a business’ financial performance and position, allowing stakeholders to gauge its profitability, solvency, and liquidity.
