Two-stage posting from special journals

An important feature of posting from special journals is that the entries are posted in two stages, namely posting to subsidiary ledger and posting to the general ledger. These are illustrated by the subsequent explanation on posting from each special journal that immediately follows.

Posting from the sales journal

The sales journal records credit sales of goods to customers. These transactions lead to the increase in revenue (sales) and increase in assets (trade receivables). Like all the other special journals, posting entries from the sales journal is done in two stages as follows:

Stage one: Posting to a subsidiary ledger

Posting to a subsidiary ledger involves posting the entries to the individual accounts of each customer who appears in the journal. The entries are recorded on the debit side of the respective accounts (personal accounts of each customer). This is because the transactions involved are the increases in assets (receivables from each respective customer).

Stage two: Posting to the general ledger

The general ledger posting involves the periodic (weekly or monthly) record of the total from the journal. These are posted to the sales account and the trade receivables account as they represent increases in revenues (sales) and trade receivables, respectively. Therefore, the trade receivables accounts will be debited, while the sales accounts will be credited.

The entries made are therefore:

Debit: Trade receivables account

Credit: Sales account

Posting from the sales returns journal

The sales returns journal records returns of goods from credit customers. These transactions lead to a decrease in revenue (sales are decreased by sales returns) and a decrease in assets (trade receivables). Like all the other special journals, posting entries from the sales returns journal is done in two stages as follows:

Stage one: Posting to a subsidiary ledger

Posting to a subsidiary ledger involves posting the entries to the individual accounts of each customer in the journal. The entries are made on the credit side of the respective accounts (personal accounts of each customer). This is because the transactions involved decreases the assets (receivables from each customer).
